# Figure contract: ASL principle and project challenges

- **Core conclusion:** ASL PWI is a weak control-label residual; using fewer repetitions shortens a lengthy 7 T examination but reduces noise suppression and corruption tolerance, while noisy targets and a sharper auxiliary T1 prior create separate reconstruction risks.
- **Figure archetype:** Schematic-led composite.
- **Target/output:** KBS double-column figure, 183 mm x 122 mm; editable-text SVG as the primary deliverable, with PDF and 600-dpi PNG companions.
- **Backend:** Python (Matplotlib) only.
- **Panel map:**
  - **a:** Control/label acquisition and endogenous arterial-water labeling.
  - **b:** Pairwise subtraction, repeated difference frames, and formation of few-frame versus 12-NEX PWI.
  - **c:** Four project challenges: weak subtraction, scan-time/reliability trade-off, no clean ground truth, and auxiliary-anatomy bias.
- **Evidence hierarchy:** The acquisition-to-PWI pipeline is the hero evidence; the challenge cards explain why few-frame self-supervised reconstruction and guarded anatomical guidance are needed.
- **Statistics/source data:** None; this is a conceptual mechanism figure rather than a quantitative result.
- **Image integrity:** All elements are schematic vector graphics generated by the plotting script. No patient image, clinical raster, or quantitative dataset is embedded.
- **Reviewer risks addressed:** Conventional positive-perfusion subtraction is stated explicitly; PWI is not labelled as CBF; the 12-NEX mean is not represented as clean ground truth; T1 is shown as a separate anatomical prior and its influence is described as a risk rather than guaranteed hallucination.
